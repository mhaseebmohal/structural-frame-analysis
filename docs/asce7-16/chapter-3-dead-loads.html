§3.1 — Dead Loads §3.1

Definition and Scope

Dead loads consist of the weight of all materials of construction incorporated into the building including, but not limited to, walls, floors, roofs, ceilings, stairways, built-in partitions, finishes, cladding, and other similarly incorporated architectural and structural items, and fixed service equipment including the weight of cranes.

  • › §3.1.1: Weight of construction — self-weight from unit weights in Table C3-1
  • › §3.1.2: Floor fills, finish, ceiling, MEP — typically specified as area loads
  • › Movable Partitions: Treated as live load when movable; treated as dead load when fixed. Minimum 0.96 kN/m² (20 psf) per §4.3.2 when movable.

§3.2 — Forces Due to Soil and Hydrostatic Pressure §3.2

Lateral Earth Pressure (H load)

In addition to the dead loads listed in §3.1, structures below grade must be designed for the lateral pressure of adjoining soil. Per §3.2, soil loads and hydrostatic pressure are classified as H loads in ASCE 7-16 §1.2.2 and enter load combinations per §2.3.1 and §2.4.1.

  • › Active earth pressure: Ka = (1 − sin φ) / (1 + sin φ)
  • › At-rest earth pressure: K₀ = 1 − sin φ (normally consolidated)
  • › Hydrostatic pressure: γ_water = 9.81 kN/m³ (62.4 pcf)
  • › H loads appear in LRFD combos with factors 1.6H (unfav.) or 0.9D + 1.6H
